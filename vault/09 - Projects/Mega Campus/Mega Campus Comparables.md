# Mega Campus Comparables

Takeaways for the campus model:
- Hyperscale shells run roughly $1,000–$1,400 per sq ft all-in at 120–130 W per gross sq ft (Vantage $2B / 1.5M sq ft / 192 MW; QTS $1.5B / 1.5M sq ft / 222 MW). The Neural Block's 60,000 sq ft at 9–21 MW is far denser per sq ft because it is an AI hall, not a leasing shell; its cost per MW in [[Mega Campus Power and Data Center Model (Oct 2026)]] is the better yardstick.
- Microsoft's $420M for 245,000 sq ft (about $1,700 per sq ft) shows what a 2026–2027 single-building AI-era build costs in Licking County.
- Land: AWS paid about $290,000 per acre for 400 acres; Intel paid about $110,000 per acre via New Albany Company. The cost model uses $110k–$290k.

## On-site power precedent

- Meta's New Albany Business Park campus is getting the Socrates South Power Generation Project by Williams: two sites of 200 MW each, built from three Solar Turbines Titan 250s, nine PGM 130s, three Siemens SGT400s and 15 Caterpillar 3520 engines, construction June 2025 to November 2026 ([Power Engineering, Onsite gas turbines, reciprocating engines to power Meta data center (June 11, 2025)](https://www.power-eng.com/onsite-power/onsite-gas-turbines-reciprocating-engines-to-power-meta-data-center/)). That is the scale of on-site gas needed to firm a GW-class AI campus; the Mega Campus CHP in the power note is 10–20 MW.

## Semiconductor and manufacturing

- Intel Ohio One, New Albany: $28 billion, two fabs (Mod 1, Mod 2), originally due end of 2026, now 2030 and 2031; 3,000 Intel jobs and 7,000 construction jobs; over 6.4 million work hours and 200,000 cubic yards of concrete by March 2025 ([Construction Dive, Intel delays Ohio chip project again to 2031 (Mar 4, 2025)](https://www.constructiondive.com/news/intel-delays-new-albany-ohio-chip-manufacturing-project-again-2030-2031/741384/)). Licking County farmland rose from $20,000/acre (2021) to $45,000 (2022) around it ([10TV, The Intel effect: Licking County land and housing prices](https://www.10tv.com/article/news/local/licking-county-land-and-housing-prices-are-rising-intel/530-f66d2315-a8e0-4fa0-833a-79ef7e6cb254)).

## Ohio solar and storage at scale

- Oak Run (Madison County): 800 MW solar + 300 MW storage on about 4,400 acres in a 6,050-acre project area, approved March 21, 2024 ([American Public Power Association, Ohio Siting Board authorizes Oak Run (Mar 2024)](https://www.publicpower.org/periodical/article/ohio-siting-board-authorizes-construction-800-mw-solar-project-300-mw-storage-facility)).
- Harvey Solar (Licking County): 350 MW on 2,630 acres; Pleasant Prairie (Franklin County): 250 MW on 2,400 acres; Flint Grid (Jersey Twp, Licking County): 200 MW / 800 MWh battery on 15 acres; all approved Oct 20, 2022 ([Ohio Power Siting Board news release (Oct 20, 2022)](https://opsb.ohio.gov/news/opsb-approves-new-energy-facilities-in-franklin-and-licking-counties-denies-solar-project-in-allen-auglaize-counties)).
- Fox Squirrel Solar (Madison County): 577 MW online Dec 2024, Ohio's largest operating plant ([Wikipedia, Solar power in Ohio (as of March 2026)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_power_in_Ohio)).

## Grid and policy context

- AEP Ohio data center tariff approved July 9, 2025: 25 MW threshold, 85% minimum demand, up to 12 years, four-year ramp ([POWER Magazine, Regulator Approves AEP Ohio's Landmark Data Center Tariff (July 2025)](https://www.powermag.com/regulator-approves-aep-ohios-landmark-data-center-tariff/); [Data Center Frontier, Ohio Sets New Precedent: AEP's Power Rules (July 23, 2025)](https://www.datacenterfrontier.com/energy/article/55304787/ohio-sets-new-precedent-aeps-power-rules-shift-data-center-cost-burden)).
- Central Ohio data center load 100 MW (2020) to 600 MW (2024), 5 GW projected by 2030; 30 GW of preliminary requests ([POWER Magazine, Regulator Approves AEP Ohio's Landmark Data Center Tariff (July 2025)](https://www.powermag.com/regulator-approves-aep-ohios-landmark-data-center-tariff/)).
- PJM: 32 GW peak growth 2024–2030, 30 GW from data centers ([DCD, PJM reports peak load growth of 30GW through 2030 from data centers (Aug 12, 2025)](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/pjm-reports-peak-load-growth-of-30gw-through-2030-from-data-center-sector/)); about 225 miles of new central Ohio transmission at nearly $2B, in service June 2029 ([Renewable Energy World, Data centers are flocking to Ohio; here comes the transmission (Jan 10, 2025)](https://www.renewableenergyworld.com/news/data-centers-are-flocking-to-ohio-here-comes-the-transmission-to-support-them/)).
- Data center market: CBRE reported record-low 6.6% vacancy across 16 primary markets and 1,668.5 MW of net absorption Q1 2024–Q1 2025; Columbus is not a tracked primary market ([DCD on CBRE, vacancy rates in top data center markets hit record low (June 26, 2025)](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/cbre-vacancy-rates-in-top-data-center-markets-hit-record-low/)).
- Columbus office: $21.23/sq ft average asking rent, 13.98% vacancy, $67.86/sq ft average sale price in 2025 ([CommercialCafe, Columbus office market trends (2025 data, Jan 2026)](https://www.commercialcafe.com/office-market-trends/us/oh/columbus/)). New HQ office at $450–$650/sq ft to build cannot be justified by Columbus market rents; the Prism is a corporate asset, not a leasing play.
